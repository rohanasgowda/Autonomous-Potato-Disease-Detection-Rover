Abstract

Plant diseases significantly affect agricultural productivity by reducing crop yield and quality, making early and accurate disease diagnosis essential for sustainable farming. Conventional disease inspection methods rely heavily on manual observation by agricultural experts, which are labor-intensive, time-consuming, and often unavailable to farmers in remote regions. Recent advances in Artificial Intelligence (AI), Deep Learning (DL), Edge Computing, and Internet of Things (IoT) technologies have enabled the development of intelligent crop monitoring systems capable of performing real-time disease detection directly in agricultural environments.

This paper presents an AI-enabled edge-based potato leaf disease detection and agricultural decision support system deployed on Raspberry Pi 5 using TensorFlow Lite optimized MobileNetV3. The proposed system performs automatic disease classification into Healthy, Early Blight, and Late Blight classes, followed by disease severity estimation through image segmentation techniques. The processed information is transmitted to a FastAPI-based backend where disease records are stored, visualized through an interactive field heatmap, and utilized for generating AI-based crop management recommendations. In addition, the platform incorporates vendor inventory management, disease history tracking, and Telegram-based notifications, thereby providing an integrated smart farming solution instead of a standalone disease classifier.

The proposed MobileNetV3 model was trained using an augmented potato leaf image dataset containing three disease categories. Experimental evaluation on an independent test dataset achieved 97.63% classification accuracy, a weighted F1-score of 97.65%, and an average inference time of 10.99 ms per image, demonstrating the suitability of the proposed framework for real-time edge deployment. The integration of lightweight deep learning, backend analytics, and decision support modules provides an efficient, scalable, and practical solution for precision agriculture.

Keywords— Precision Agriculture, Plant Disease Detection, MobileNetV3, TensorFlow Lite, Raspberry Pi 5, Edge AI, Deep Learning, Severity Estimation, FastAPI, Smart Agriculture.

I. INTRODUCTION

Agriculture continues to play a vital role in ensuring global food security and economic development. However, plant diseases remain one of the major factors responsible for substantial crop losses worldwide, resulting in reduced productivity, poor crop quality, and increased dependence on chemical pesticides. Among various agricultural crops, potato is highly susceptible to fungal diseases such as Early Blight and Late Blight, both of which spread rapidly under favorable environmental conditions and significantly affect crop yield if not detected at an early stage.

Traditional disease identification primarily depends on manual visual inspection performed by experienced agricultural professionals. Although effective under controlled conditions, manual diagnosis suffers from several limitations including subjective interpretation, delayed detection, high labor requirements, and limited accessibility in rural farming regions. Consequently, there is an increasing demand for intelligent automated systems capable of providing rapid, accurate, and real-time disease diagnosis directly in agricultural fields.

Recent developments in Artificial Intelligence (AI), Deep Learning (DL), Computer Vision, Edge Computing, and Internet of Things (IoT) technologies have transformed modern agricultural monitoring systems. Lightweight convolutional neural networks such as MobileNetV3 enable efficient disease classification while maintaining low computational complexity, making them suitable for deployment on resource-constrained edge devices including Raspberry Pi. Edge computing further reduces network dependency by performing inference locally, thereby minimizing latency and improving system reliability in remote agricultural environments.

Several researchers have proposed deep learning-based disease detection systems for different crops. However, many existing approaches focus exclusively on disease classification and fail to provide comprehensive decision support functionalities required for practical agricultural deployment. Features such as disease severity estimation, backend database management, intelligent treatment recommendation, inventory tracking, field-wise visualization, and farmer notification mechanisms are often absent.

To address these limitations, this paper proposes an integrated AI-enabled smart agricultural platform capable of performing disease detection, severity estimation, automated recommendation generation, vendor inventory management, interactive field heatmap visualization, and real-time Telegram notifications within a unified architecture. The proposed framework combines a TensorFlow Lite optimized MobileNetV3 classifier deployed on Raspberry Pi 5 with a FastAPI backend and interactive web dashboard, enabling practical deployment for precision agriculture applications.

The major contributions of this work are summarized as follows:

Development of a lightweight MobileNetV3-based potato leaf disease classification model optimized for Raspberry Pi 5 using TensorFlow Lite.
Integration of disease severity estimation using image segmentation for quantitative assessment of infected leaf regions.
Design of a FastAPI backend supporting disease record management, AI-based recommendation generation, vendor inventory tracking, and field-wise heatmap visualization.
Deployment of a complete edge-enabled agricultural decision support system incorporating Telegram-based farmer notifications.
Experimental validation demonstrating high classification accuracy (97.63%) and real-time inference performance suitable for practical field applications.

# **II. RELATED WORK**

The rapid advancement of Artificial Intelligence (AI), Deep Learning (DL), Edge Computing, and Internet of Things (IoT) technologies has significantly transformed modern agricultural monitoring systems. Recent research has focused on developing intelligent crop disease detection frameworks capable of improving disease diagnosis accuracy while minimizing manual intervention. However, most existing solutions concentrate primarily on disease classification and provide limited support for complete agricultural decision-making. This section reviews recent literature related to autonomous crop monitoring, plant disease classification, edge intelligence, and smart agriculture.

Zhao *et al.* [1] proposed a fully autonomous robotic inspection system for detecting strawberry pests and diseases in protected cultivation environments. The system combined autonomous navigation with intelligent image acquisition to automate greenhouse inspection. Although the proposed robotic platform effectively reduced manual monitoring efforts, its application was limited to controlled cultivation environments and did not incorporate disease severity estimation, farmer notification, backend analytics, or inventory management modules required for practical agricultural deployment.

Pratiarso *et al.* [2] presented an aerial deep learning-based disease detection framework for rice fields using unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) mapping techniques. High-resolution aerial images were processed using convolutional neural networks to identify pest and disease affected regions over large agricultural fields. While the system demonstrated efficient large-scale monitoring capability, it relied heavily on aerial imaging infrastructure and did not provide edge deployment or real-time farmer assistance features suitable for small and medium-scale farms.

Karri *et al.* [3] introduced an intelligent brinjal disease detection system integrated with fertilizer recommendation functionality. Their framework combined deep learning-based disease identification with agricultural advisory generation to assist farmers in selecting appropriate treatments. Although recommendation support was incorporated, the system lacked disease severity estimation, edge-device implementation, interactive visualization, and centralized backend management for continuous crop monitoring.

Cunha *et al.* [4] investigated multispectral image segmentation techniques for agricultural applications by comparing multiple image fusion approaches. Their comprehensive study demonstrated that multispectral imaging significantly improves segmentation performance under varying environmental conditions. However, the requirement for specialized imaging hardware limits practical deployment in low-cost agricultural systems where conventional RGB cameras remain the preferred solution.

Guo *et al.* [5] presented a comprehensive survey on attention mechanisms in computer vision, discussing various attention-based deep learning architectures that enhance feature extraction and classification performance. Although attention mechanisms improve model accuracy for complex vision tasks, many attention-based networks possess higher computational complexity, making them less suitable for deployment on low-power embedded platforms such as Raspberry Pi without significant optimization.

Yeaser *et al.* [6] developed an IoT-based smart agriculture monitoring and automated irrigation system integrating environmental sensors with wireless communication technologies. Their work demonstrated the effectiveness of IoT for improving resource utilization and automated irrigation control. However, disease diagnosis capabilities were not incorporated, limiting the system's ability to perform intelligent crop health monitoring.

Lanjewar *et al.* [7] proposed modified transfer learning frameworks for potato leaf disease identification using lightweight convolutional neural networks. Their comparative study demonstrated that transfer learning significantly improves classification performance while reducing training complexity. Nevertheless, the work primarily focused on classification accuracy and did not extend toward real-time deployment, disease severity estimation, backend integration, or intelligent agricultural decision support.

Paul *et al.* [8] compared multiple deep learning architectures for potato leaf disease detection using Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) techniques. Their research highlighted the importance of model interpretability for improving user confidence in AI-based agricultural systems. While explainability provides valuable insights into model decisions, the proposed framework remained limited to offline disease classification without integration into a complete agricultural monitoring platform.

Sharma *et al.* [9] introduced an edge AI-based IoT platform for real-time plant disease detection using embedded computing devices. The proposed architecture demonstrated the feasibility of deploying lightweight deep learning models on resource-constrained edge hardware for low-latency disease diagnosis. However, additional functionalities such as disease severity estimation, centralized database management, vendor assistance, and field visualization were not addressed.

Kruthi *et al.* [10] designed an AI-powered plant disease detection and remediation framework based on the YOLOv5 object detection model. Their system successfully combined object detection with automated remediation strategies for precision agriculture. Although YOLOv5 provides accurate localization capabilities, the computational requirements remain relatively higher than lightweight classification networks, making deployment on embedded edge devices comparatively challenging.

The above studies clearly demonstrate the growing importance of AI-driven crop disease detection systems. Nevertheless, most existing works emphasize either disease classification, autonomous monitoring, aerial imaging, or IoT-based sensing independently. Very few systems integrate lightweight edge intelligence with disease severity estimation, backend analytics, interactive field visualization, intelligent recommendation generation, vendor inventory management, and real-time farmer notification within a single unified framework.

To address these research gaps, the proposed work develops an integrated AI-enabled agricultural decision support platform that combines TensorFlow Lite optimized MobileNetV3 disease classification, image-based severity estimation, FastAPI backend services, SQLite database management, interactive heatmap visualization, AI-powered treatment recommendations, vendor inventory support, and Telegram notifications on a Raspberry Pi 5 edge computing platform. The proposed architecture provides a practical, scalable, and comprehensive solution suitable for real-world precision agriculture applications.

## **III. PROPOSED METHODOLOGY**

The proposed system is designed as a comprehensive AI-enabled smart agricultural platform capable of performing real-time potato leaf disease detection, disease severity estimation, intelligent recommendation generation, vendor inventory management, interactive field visualization, and automated farmer notification. Unlike conventional disease classification systems, the proposed framework integrates lightweight deep learning, edge computing, backend analytics, and decision support functionalities within a unified architecture suitable for practical agricultural deployment.

The complete system consists of six major functional modules: (i) image acquisition and preprocessing, (ii) MobileNetV3-based disease classification, (iii) disease severity estimation, (iv) FastAPI backend with database management, (v) intelligent decision support modules, and (vi) interactive web dashboard with notification services. The overall workflow enables disease diagnosis directly on a Raspberry Pi 5 while simultaneously synchronizing processed information with the backend server for visualization and long-term monitoring.

### **A. Image Acquisition and Preprocessing**

The image acquisition module captures potato leaf images using a Raspberry Pi Camera connected to the Raspberry Pi 5. Images may also be uploaded manually through the web interface for disease analysis. Prior to classification, each image undergoes preprocessing operations to improve input consistency and enhance model performance.

The preprocessing stage includes image resizing to **224 × 224 pixels**, RGB color normalization, and automatic augmentation during model training. Augmentation techniques include random horizontal flipping, rotation, zooming, brightness adjustment, and translation, thereby improving model robustness against variations in illumination, viewing angle, and field conditions while reducing overfitting.

---

### **B. MobileNetV3-Based Disease Classification**

Disease classification is performed using **MobileNetV3 Small**, a lightweight convolutional neural network optimized for embedded edge devices. MobileNetV3 combines depthwise separable convolutions, squeeze-and-excitation attention modules, and efficient nonlinear activation functions to achieve high classification accuracy with minimal computational overhead.

Transfer learning is employed by initializing the backbone with ImageNet pretrained weights, after which the final classification layer is fine-tuned using the potato leaf disease dataset. The classifier predicts one of three disease categories:

* Healthy
* Early Blight
* Late Blight

The final output layer utilizes the Softmax activation function to compute class probabilities, enabling confident disease prediction suitable for real-time agricultural applications.

---

### **C. Disease Severity Estimation**

Following disease classification, the infected leaf region is quantified through an image segmentation-based severity estimation algorithm. The proposed method isolates diseased regions by combining color-space thresholding, morphological filtering, contour extraction, and pixel-area analysis.

The infected area percentage is calculated using

[
Severity(%)=\frac{\text{Number of Diseased Pixels}}{\text{Total Leaf Pixels}}\times100
]

The computed severity value is categorized into multiple infection levels, allowing farmers to assess disease progression and prioritize treatment decisions.

Severity estimation provides additional information beyond disease classification by measuring the extent of infection rather than only identifying the disease category.

### **D. FastAPI Backend and Database Management**

The backend is implemented using the **FastAPI** framework to provide efficient communication between the Raspberry Pi edge device, web dashboard, and database. FastAPI REST APIs manage image uploads, disease prediction requests, severity calculation, recommendation retrieval, inventory management, and heatmap generation.

All disease records are stored in a **SQLite** relational database, enabling historical analysis and visualization of disease progression over time.

The database maintains information including:

* Image path
* Disease class
* Prediction confidence
* Severity percentage
* Timestamp
* Plant location
* Recommendation status

The backend architecture supports modular development and enables future migration to cloud-based database systems without major architectural modifications.

### **E. Performance Evaluation Metrics**

The proposed model was evaluated using standard multi-class classification metrics commonly employed in deep learning-based image classification.

**Accuracy** measures the percentage of correctly classified samples over the total number of testing samples.

[
Accuracy=\frac{TP+TN}{TP+TN+FP+FN}
]

**Precision** indicates the proportion of correctly predicted positive samples among all predicted positive samples.

[
Precision=\frac{TP}{TP+FP}
]

**Recall** represents the proportion of actual positive samples correctly identified by the classifier.

[
Recall=\frac{TP}{TP+FN}
]

**F1-score** provides a balanced evaluation by combining precision and recall.

[
F1=\frac{2\times Precision\times Recall}{Precision+Recall}
]

In addition to classification metrics, the average inference time per image was measured to evaluate real-time deployment capability on edge hardware.

The MobileNetV3 classifier demonstrated excellent performance across all disease categories. The model achieved an overall classification accuracy of **97.63%** on the independent test dataset, indicating strong generalization capability despite variations introduced through image augmentation during training.

The high classification accuracy can be attributed to the efficient feature extraction capability of MobileNetV3 combined with transfer learning from ImageNet pretrained weights. Furthermore, lightweight depthwise separable convolutions enabled efficient deployment on Raspberry Pi without sacrificing prediction performance.

# **VI. CONCLUSION**

This paper presented an AI-enabled edge-based smart agricultural decision support system for automatic potato leaf disease detection, severity estimation, and intelligent crop management. The proposed framework combines a lightweight MobileNetV3 deep learning model with TensorFlow Lite optimization, enabling efficient deployment on Raspberry Pi 5 for real-time field applications. Unlike conventional disease classification systems, the proposed platform integrates disease severity estimation, FastAPI-based backend services, SQLite database management, interactive heatmap visualization, AI-driven treatment recommendation, vendor inventory management, and Telegram-based notification services into a unified agricultural monitoring ecosystem.

Experimental evaluation performed on an independent potato leaf test dataset demonstrated the effectiveness of the proposed approach. The MobileNetV3 classifier achieved an overall classification accuracy of **97.63%**, with a weighted F1-score of **97.65%** and an average inference time of **10.99 ms per image**, confirming its suitability for resource-constrained edge devices. The disease severity estimation module further enhanced the system by quantifying the infected leaf area, enabling informed treatment prioritization rather than relying solely on disease classification. Additionally, the integrated dashboard, recommendation engine, and inventory management modules provide farmers with actionable insights for timely crop management.

The proposed system demonstrates that combining lightweight deep learning, edge computing, backend analytics, and intelligent decision support can significantly improve practical agricultural disease monitoring. The developed framework is scalable, cost-effective, and suitable for real-world precision agriculture, particularly in regions where continuous internet connectivity and expert consultation may not always be available.

# **VII. FUTURE WORK**

Although the proposed framework demonstrates promising performance, several improvements can further enhance its applicability and scalability.

Future work may include:

* Extension of the classification model to support multiple crop species and a larger number of plant diseases.
* Integration of environmental sensors such as temperature, humidity, and soil moisture for multimodal crop health monitoring.
* Implementation of Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) techniques such as Grad-CAM to improve model interpretability.
* Migration from SQLite to cloud-based database systems for large-scale agricultural deployment.
* Development of a dedicated Android mobile application for remote disease diagnosis and monitoring.
* Integration of GPS-based field mapping for geospatial disease visualization.
* Continuous disease progression monitoring using drone and satellite imagery.
* Incorporation of predictive analytics for disease outbreak forecasting using historical agricultural data.
* Multi-language recommendation support to improve accessibility for farmers across different regions.
* Integration with automated precision spraying systems for targeted pesticide application.

These enhancements would transform the proposed framework into a comprehensive smart farming ecosystem capable of supporting large-scale precision agriculture and sustainable crop management.
